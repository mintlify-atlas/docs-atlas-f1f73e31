---
title: "Bench press: elbow flare angle rules and detection"
description: "GymGuard measures the hip-shoulder-elbow angle on each side during bench press and flags elbow flare when either arm exceeds 80 degrees of abduction."
---

When GymGuard detects a bench press, it activates `bench.analyze()` to measure how far your elbows are flaring away from your torso. The analyzer calculates the angle between your hip, shoulder, and elbow on each side — a proxy for elbow abduction. Flaring the elbows too wide places excessive stress on the rotator cuff and anterior shoulder capsule, increasing the risk of tears under load.

## How bench press detection works

GymGuard classifies your pose as a bench press when your hips are above your shoulders in the camera frame — indicating you are lying on a bench with the camera oriented toward you. A 12-frame rolling buffer prevents brief postural shifts from misclassifying the exercise.

## Joint angles tracked

| Angle name | Keypoints used | Stored as |
|---|---|---|
| Left elbow flare | left_hip → left_shoulder → left_elbow | `L_flare` |
| Right elbow flare | right_hip → right_shoulder → right_elbow | `R_flare` |

The flare angle represents how far your elbow moves away from your body relative to your shoulder. An angle of 0° would mean the elbow is tucked directly to your side; larger angles mean the elbow is flaring wider.

```python
l_flare = angle_between(lh, ls, le)
r_flare = angle_between(rh, rs, re)
```

## Form issues detected

### Elbow flare

| Issue | Trigger condition | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Left Elbow Flare | `L_flare > 80°` | Left elbow too wide - rotator cuff risk |
| Right Elbow Flare | `R_flare > 80°` | Right elbow too wide - rotator cuff risk |

```python
if l_flare and l_flare > 80:
    issues.append({
        "name": "Left Elbow Flare",
        "severity": round(min(1.0, (l_flare - 80) / 40), 2),
        "message": "Left elbow too wide - rotator cuff risk"
    })
if r_flare and r_flare > 80:
    issues.append({
        "name": "Right Elbow Flare",
        "severity": round(min(1.0, (r_flare - 80) / 40), 2),
        "message": "Right elbow too wide - rotator cuff risk"
    })
```

**Severity formula:** `min(1.0, (flare_angle - 80) / 40)` — severity reaches 1.0 when your elbow flare angle hits 120°, which represents extreme abduction well past a safe range.

<Warning>
Elbow flare under a loaded bar places the shoulder in a vulnerable externally rotated and abducted position. This is a known mechanism for rotator cuff tears and anterior labral injuries. If you see this flagged at HIGH RISK, reduce the weight and consciously tuck your elbows to roughly 45–75° of abduction.
</Warning>

## Good form vs. flagged form

| What GymGuard sees | State |
|---|---|
| Flare angle ≤ 80° | No flag — elbow angle within safe range |
| Flare angle 80–120° | Elbow Flare flagged — severity scales with abduction |
| Flare angle ≥ 120° | Severity 1.0 — maximum flare risk |

## Elbow angle asymmetry

GymGuard checks left and right independently, so it can catch unilateral issues — for example, a right elbow flaring while the left stays tucked. Both sides are stored in the `angles` dict and displayed on the dashboard so you can compare them between sets.

<Note>
The ideal elbow angle for bench press depends on your goal and grip width. A competition powerlifting setup may use a 45° tuck; a hypertrophy-focused setup often allows slightly more flare. GymGuard's 80° threshold is a conservative safety boundary suited to most training styles.
</Note>

<Tip>
Think about "bending the bar" or "screwing your hands into the bar" before you unrack. This external rotation cue naturally positions the elbows at a safe angle and engages the lats, which stabilize the shoulder through the press.
</Tip>
